## 1. Introduction

Blockchain is a distributed digital ledger technology used to store information securely across multiple computers in a network. The data is stored in blocks, and these blocks are connected using cryptographic techniques to form a chain.

Unlike traditional databases, blockchain can provide decentralization, transparency, security and data integrity.

Blockchain technology became popular through Bitcoin, but today it is used in banking, healthcare, supply chain management, education, government services and many other industries.

Main features of Blockchain:

1. Decentralization: Data is distributed across multiple computers instead of being controlled by one central authority.
2. Transparency: Participants can verify transactions according to network permissions.
3. Security: Cryptography protects transactions and data.
4. Immutability: Confirmed records are difficult to modify or delete.
5. Traceability: Transactions can be tracked and verified.
6. Consensus Mechanism: Network participants follow an agreed method to validate transactions.

## 2. Types of Blockchains

Blockchain technology is mainly classified into four types:

1\. Public Blockchain

A public blockchain is an open and decentralized network where anyone can join, view transactions and participate according to the network rules.

Examples: Bitcoin and Ethereum.

2\. Private Blockchain

A private blockchain is controlled by a single organization. Only authorized users can access and perform transactions.

Examples: Hyperledger Fabric deployments and private enterprise blockchain networks.

3\. Consortium Blockchain

A consortium blockchain is managed by multiple organizations instead of a single organization. Access is restricted to approved participants.

Examples: Multi-bank blockchain networks and trade finance consortiums.

4\. Hybrid Blockchain

A hybrid blockchain combines public and private blockchain features. Some information remains private, while selected information can be made publicly verifiable.

Examples: Enterprise systems combining private records with public blockchain verification.

## 5. Advantages of Blockchain Technology

1. High Security: Cryptographic techniques protect stored information.
2. Transparency: Transactions can be verified by authorized participants.
3. Reduced Intermediaries: Some transactions can be completed without third-party involvement.
4. Data Integrity: Previously confirmed records are difficult to modify.
5. Traceability: Every recorded transaction can be tracked.
6. Automation: Smart contracts automatically execute predefined conditions.

## 6. Limitations of Blockchain Technology

1. Scalability: Some blockchain networks process fewer transactions per second than traditional databases.
2. High Transaction Fees: Public networks may charge high transaction fees during heavy network usage.
3. Energy Consumption: Some consensus mechanisms, such as Proof of Work, consume significant electricity.
4. Complexity: Blockchain development and maintenance require specialized knowledge.
5. Privacy Issues: Public blockchains can expose transaction information.
6. Regulatory Challenges: Cryptocurrency and blockchain regulations differ between countries.

## 10. Conclusion

Blockchain is an emerging technology that provides secure, transparent and decentralized methods of storing and sharing information. Today, blockchain is being adopted in various industries, including banking, healthcare, education, supply chain management, real estate and government services. Although it has certain limitations, such as scalability, transaction costs and regulatory challenges, it has significant potential to transform digital services in the future.
